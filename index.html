Digimon World 2 (PS1): Digivolve Guide

How to use thisWhat each part does
  • Quick finder: type any two Digimon, even at different levels, and it shows what they make. Type just one and press Possible combos to see everything it can make, shown as a family tree.
  • Find a Parent Digimon in the chart: type a Champion, Ultimate or Mega name to light up its row and column in gold.
  • Reverse search: type the Digimon you want and see every pair of parents that makes it.
  • Type colors: green is Vaccine, blue is Data and red is Virus, shown on names, letters and headers.
  • Chart switcher: choose Champion + Champion (makes a Rookie), Ultimate + Ultimate (makes a Champion) or Mega + Mega (makes an Ultimate).
  • Row and column letters: each letter is a group of Digimon that give the same result; hover or tap one to see who is in it.
  • Cells: the big name is the Digimon you get, and the small letters underneath are the two groups that made it.
  • Box above the chart: tap any cell to list the exact parent Digimon behind it.
  • Group list below the chart: shows every Digimon in each letter group.

Digivolution family trees

Each tree starts at a Rookie and branches into the Champions, Ultimates and Megas it can become. The DP badge is the Digivolve Points needed for that branch; the game picks the branch that matches your Digimon’s DP. DP goes up by 1 each time you DNA Digivolve. Digivolving needs EL 11 (Rookie to Champion), EL 21 (Champion to Ultimate) and EL 31 (Ultimate to Mega).

Digimon Quality calculator

Quality (0 to 100) sets how well a Digimon’s stats grow, so higher is better. You can’t see it on wild Digimon, because it is decided the moment one joins your party. Digimon with 95 to 100 Quality have a foil effect. Enter the two Digimon you plan to DNA Digivolve to see the new Digimon’s max level and Quality.

Max level: the higher level + (the lower level ÷ 5), rounded down. Example: levels 15 and 13 give 15 + 2 = 17. Levels 13 and 17 give 17 + 2 = 19, whichever slot each is in (tested in game).

Quality: the new Digimon inherits the Quality of the higher-level parent, and order doesn’t matter. If both parents are the same level, it inherits the higher Quality. Examples: level 15 with Quality 66 and level 12 with Quality 87 gives 66. Level 13 with Quality 66 and level 15 with Quality 87 gives 87. Level 15 with Quality 66 and level 15 with Quality 87 gives 87.

Digimon Moves

Every move, with the stage it belongs to, what it costs, how hard it hits and any side effect. A Digimon learns moves of its own stage, so the Stage column tells you when it comes online. Pick a stage, kind or status, search a move or Digimon, or tap a Digimon name in the table to see just its moves.

Damage formula (approximate, because the game shows rounded stats):

damage = floor( floor(Type × Specialty × Power × MoveTile) × Attack ÷ floor(Defense × DefenderTile) )
  • Type: 1.2 when the attacker has the advantage (Virus beats Data, Data beats Vaccine, Vaccine beats Virus), 0.8 when at a disadvantage, otherwise 1.0.
  • Specialty: 1.2, 1.0 or 0.8 from the move’s element against the target’s (for example a Water move against a Fire Digimon is 1.2).
  • Power: the move’s listed power. MoveTile: 1.2, 1.0 or 0.8 for the tile under the attacker matching the move’s element (a Darkness move on a Darkness tile is 1.2).
  • Attack / Defense: the attacker’s Attack and the target’s Defense stats. DefenderTile: the same 1.2, 1.0 or 0.8 idea, for the target’s own specialty on its tile.

Stat-down effects (the Debuff moves that hit all enemies):

  • Duration: they now last 3 rounds, not the whole battle.
  • No stacking: hitting a stat that is already lowered just restarts the 3 rounds, so two Okuwamon give the same effect as one.
  • Bosses: they only get half the effect, and a boss stat can never drop below 75% of its original value.

Power shows “–” for moves that don’t deal damage. “Unknown” learners are moves only seen on bosses or wild Digimon. Move data from a community skill list; effects are summarised in my own words.
